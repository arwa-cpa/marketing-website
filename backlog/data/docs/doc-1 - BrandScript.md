The StoryBrand BrandScript for Arbur CPA: the message the site's copy is built from. The client is the hero; Arwa is the guide. It's a living document: when the message changes, change it here first, then the site.

The message in one line: Arwa makes your cross-border taxes understandable, by simplifying what applies to you and explaining it thoroughly. Lead with that, not with detail.

## 1. Character

- **Who:** individuals and families who are making moves across the U.S.–Canada border: relocating, marrying, building careers or businesses on both sides. Life is going well, and they now have income, assets or a business in both countries.
- **What they want:** to move forward confidently, knowing they're paying the right amount and reporting everything correctly in both countries, so they can focus on their work, their investments and a rich life, not on accounting.
- **The wider circle:** the same thing happens without crossing a border. A growing business, rental properties or investments can slowly turn simple finances into complicated ones. Cross-border clients are the focus, but the skill that makes cross-border taxes understandable, the most complicated kind, works for any client whose success has made their taxes harder. The site leads with cross-border (Arwa as the specialist); these clients are welcomed through Other services.

## 2. Problem

- **Villain:** two tax systems that don't talk to each other. Every move adds forms, deadlines and rules in both countries, and nobody tells you which ones now apply to you.
- **External:** income, accounts and property on both sides mean filing in two countries, with extra reporting on foreign accounts and assets that's easy to miss, and a real risk of paying tax twice.
- **Internal:** a low, constant worry. Am I missing something? Am I paying too much? It takes time and attention away from the life they're building.
- **Philosophical:** success shouldn't come with a tax headache. Building a life across two countries should feel like an achievement, not a liability.

## 3. Guide: Arwa

- **Empathy:** Arwa holds both U.S. and Canadian citizenship and has lived and travelled between the two countries all her life. She knows both places, and what it's like when both tax systems apply to you.
- **Authority:**
  - a CPA licensed in both Michigan and Ontario
  - experience on cross-border tax teams at public accounting firms
  - serves many clients with complex cross-border situations
- **How she guides:** she makes your taxes understandable, simplifying what applies to you and explaining it thoroughly. And the work doesn't end at filing: she makes sure you understand your finances and have what you need to keep them in order, so getting it right this year keeps paying off.

## 4. Plan

1. **Book a consultation.** Tell Arwa where you live, work and own things, and what's changed.
2. **Agree on a plan.** She explains what applies to you in both countries, and puts what she'll do for you in writing before any work starts.
3. **Stay in order.** She gets your filings right, and sets you up so things stay that way, with a clear grasp of your finances, not a one-time fix.

The paperwork (engagement letter, statement of work, any retainer) and how hands-on the ongoing support is (a system the client runs, hands-on setup, or ongoing bookkeeping) vary by client, so the plan states the intent and leaves those details for the consultation.

## 5. Call to action

- **Direct:** Book a consultation.
- **Transitional:** Have a question first? Send a short note through the contact form, or call or text.

## 6. Failure (what's at stake)

Without the right help, the moves that should feel like progress turn into a burden. That means penalties for forms nobody told you about, paying tax twice on the same income, and years of nagging uncertainty about whether it's all correct. Hours go to paperwork instead of to the life you're building. And as you keep growing, the burden grows with you.

## 7. Success

You know where you stand in both countries. You're paying the right amount and reporting everything correctly, your finances are in order and stay that way, and you understand them. Each new move, whether a job, a property, a business or a marriage, feels like progress again, not paperwork. You can focus on living a rich life, not accounting for it.
